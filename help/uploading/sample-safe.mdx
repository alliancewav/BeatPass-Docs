The **Sample-Safe™ warranty** section tells BeatPass whether your beat is fully cleared or whether a buyer should expect extra sample-clearance work.

This is producer self-certification. BeatPass relies on the status you choose when showing sample-related warnings to buyers.

<Warning>
  Sample-Safe is about sample clearance. It does not replace BeatPass's AI Audio Policy, human-made beat requirement, AI disclosure obligations, or Proof of Rights requirements for AI-generated elements.
</Warning>


## Choose the sample status

<SampleSafeFigure />

In **Protection & Verification**, choose:

<Columns cols={2}>
  <Card title="Cleared" icon="shield">
    Use this when the beat is 100% original or all samples are fully cleared.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Needs clearance" icon="triangle-exclamation">
    Use this when the beat includes samples that still require outside clearance.
  </Card>
</Columns>

## How to Set It

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open Protection & Verification">
    On the track form, go to the section that includes **Sample-Safe™ warranty**.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Choose the status card">
    Click either **Cleared** or **Needs clearance**.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Add notes if needed">
    BeatPass only shows the **Sample details** field when you choose **Needs clearance**.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Save the track">
    Your selected state is saved with the beat.
  </Step>
</Steps>



## What Buyers See

Your saved choice tells buyers:

- **Cleared** → BeatPass shows the beat as sample-safe certified
- **Needs clearance** → BeatPass shows a buyer-facing clearance warning

The warning does not grant missing rights. Buyers must resolve the disclosed clearance issue before using material that needs permission.

## What Counts as Cleared?

Choose **Cleared** only when one of these is true:

- The beat is built entirely from original material
- All sampled material is properly licensed or cleared
- Your permission covers licensing the material to BeatPass customers for the uses you offer

## What Counts as Needs clearance?

Choose **Needs clearance** when:

- You sampled another recording or composition without full clearance
- You used material that could trigger outside permissions
- You are unsure whether the sample use is fully cleared

<Warning>
  When in doubt, **Needs clearance** is the safer choice. Mislabeling a beat as cleared can create legal and business problems for both you and the buyer.
</Warning>

## Notes for Uncleared Samples

If you choose **Needs clearance**, use the notes field to add context such as:

- What type of sample was used
- Whether clearance is in progress
- Anything the buyer should understand before release

Keep the note short and practical.

## Best Practices

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Document your sample sources">
    Keep receipts, licenses, and written permissions for sample packs and cleared content.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Update the status if things change">
    If you later obtain all required permissions, update an unsold beat from **Needs clearance** to **Cleared**. For a sold beat with locked details, contact support with the track link and evidence.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Do not treat this as legal advice">
    Sample clearance rules vary by situation. BeatPass helps surface the issue, but you are still responsible for your own sample use.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

## How This Differs From AI Use

Sample-Safe and the AI use declaration are separate checks:

| Area | What it answers |
| --- | --- |
| **Sample-Safe** | Are samples original, cleared, or still needing outside clearance? |
| **AI use declaration** | Was AI used, does the beat remain human-made, and can the producer prove commercial-use rights for AI material? |

Choosing **Needs clearance** for sample status does not make unverifiable AI content acceptable. If an AI sample, loop, vocal, stem, or generated source cannot be verified or licensed through BeatPass, it should not remain in your catalog.

See [AI Audio and Human-Made Content](/help/legal/ai-audio-human-made-content) for the AI policy.
