A BeatPass license certificate records your right to use a track under a specific license. It combines track details, your license identity, the usage terms, and a public verification link.

BeatPass issues certificates based on producer warranties, disclosures, and Proof of Rights. A certificate documents the license record, but it does not guarantee that no third-party claim, AI-origin dispute, or rights-verification issue can ever arise.

## Check the terms for your license

Read your certificate with the [Terms of Service](https://open.beatpass.ca/pages/terms-of-service), the applicable agreement, and your transaction or signed terms. The [standard license guide](/help/legal/license-terms/non-exclusive) explains the current one-project limits and income-sharing obligations; exclusive rights depend on the individual offer. This guide does not change an earlier grant.

## How to Access a Certificate

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open Your licenses">
    Open [Library → My Licenses](https://open.beatpass.ca/library/licenses). The page is titled **Your licenses**.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Find the license">
    Search by track, artist, licensee, or license ID.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Open the certificate">
    Select **Download** on the license card to open the printable certificate page for that license.
  </Step>
</Steps>



### Other Ways to Reach the Certificate

- **My licenses** in the **Download requested** dialog after requesting the audio
- A saved link to that certificate

<Note>
  An eligible non-owner download can generate a license certificate automatically. A repeat download may return the existing valid certificate rather than create another project license.
</Note>

## What the Certificate Shows

The printable document records the certificate ID, license type, licensed party, beat, producer identities, issued terms, and verification link. Exclusive documents also include purchase and acceptance evidence when recorded.

**Audio fingerprint evidence** can include the saved audio hash when available. A missing fingerprint is different from proof that the underlying rights do not exist. Ask support about a record that cannot be opened or checked.

## Names and License Cards

Use your complete legal first and last name for the certificate. Your public handle stays separate. Customers use **Review legal name** and **Confirm and lock name**; producers follow **Review payout details** or their Finances setup. [Account details](/help/account-settings/account-details) explains locked-name corrections.

When missing identity details are saved, BeatPass automatically completes eligible certificates. An older record may still need producer-credit review or support. Follow the action on the card rather than assuming every missing-name record needs another manual confirmation.

If another person needs to act, finish your own step first. Use **Message producer** or **Ask license holder** when offered, review the message, and send it yourself. A saved draft is preserved.

The card shows the beat, licensed party, license type, and status. Open **Details** for the issued date and certificate ID, then choose **Verify** inside that dialog for a public status check. Use **Download** when the certificate is ready. Text labels explain status without relying on colour.

A technical identity or certificate problem does not itself revoke an original grant. Original records, signed names, and issued terms remain part of the history. A separately issued replacement has its own record; use the new certificate when directed and keep the original for your records.

## Certificate vs receipt vs download history

A payment receipt proves that a transaction happened. Download history shows that an account accessed a file.

A BeatPass license certificate records the license identity, track details, applicable usage terms, licensed-user verification, fingerprint details when available, and a public verification link. When a distributor, platform, collaborator, label, or rights team asks for proof, the certificate is the document designed for that moment.

## AI and Rights-Issue Context

Certificates do not include AI training, datasets, sample packs, or competing-library use without separate written permission. BeatPass relies on producer rights and declarations; it cannot grant rights the producer did not own.

If a verified rights issue arises, support may review documentation, credits, replacements, or a refund under the [Refund Policy](/help/billing/refund-policy). A monetary refund or third-party clearance is not guaranteed.

## Standard Non-Exclusive Terms

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="What you can do" icon="circle-check" defaultOpen>
    Create one original musical work with the beat and credit the producer. Follow the applicable limits: the standard agreement allows 100,000 physical or digital units, 500,000 streams, and one music video no longer than five minutes. The current standard certificate limits live events to 50 attendees per event; use your issued terms for an earlier grant. Performance royalties and synchronization income default to 50/50 between producer and licensee unless the transaction terms, certificate, or a separate agreement provide otherwise.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="What you cannot do" icon="circle-xmark">
    Release the beat by itself, use it in film/TV/broadcast radio/commercials/video games, perform at events exceeding the licensed attendance limit, claim ownership of the beat, resell or share the beat or stems, or register the beat in Content ID.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Attribution" icon="signature">
    Standard non-exclusive terms require credit in this format: **Produced by [Producer Name]**.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

## Common questions

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Can I send my certificate to a distributor?" icon="paper-plane" defaultOpen>
    Yes. If a distributor, label, platform, or rights team asks for proof, send the certificate or its public verification link.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Does my certificate still matter if I cancel?" icon="infinity">
    Yes, for licenses that remain valid under the applicable terms. Cancellation affects future access, not the existence of an already issued certificate.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Is a screenshot enough?" icon="camera">
    Use the certificate and public verification link instead. A screenshot cannot independently confirm the certificate's current status.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

## Exclusive Certificate Terms

Your exclusive certificate records the terms for that purchase. Check it with the preview, checkout terms, governing Terms, applicable agreement and any signed addendum.

Confirm the rights granted, license duration, files, producer credit, Content ID permission, transfer restrictions, and any publishing or royalty obligations. These depend on the purchase. Do not assume a generic default percentage applies to your license.

## Printing and Sharing

The certificate opens as a printable BeatPass page. In practice, you can:

- Print it or save it as a PDF from your browser
- Share the public verification URL when a platform, distributor, or rights team needs confirmation

## Status and Priority Rules

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Active vs revoked" icon="shield-halved" defaultOpen>
    The certificate and public verification page show the current recorded status. Technical integrity, missing-data or expiry checks can affect certificate display or printing. A certificate check failure alone does not establish that a perpetual legal grant has expired. Keep the license record and contact support to clarify the issue.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Rights issue or review status" icon="triangle-exclamation">
    Read the explanation shown with the certificate and contact support when it asks for review. A review result does not itself show that the buyer acted wrongly, and a technical certificate issue does not by itself revoke the original grant.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Exclusive vs non-exclusive priority" icon="crown">
    An exclusive purchase stops future licensing to new buyers while earlier valid non-exclusive licenses continue under their applicable terms. Interface display priority does not cancel those earlier rights. Exclusive licenses are colour-coded blue; non-exclusive licenses are green.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Producer view" icon="user">
    If you own tracks, **Your licenses** also shows licenses granted to other users for your catalog.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>
